A softer read

Loose usually means comfortable.

A relaxed dog can still be alert or excited. Look for easy movement and the ability to choose what happens next.

  • Look forSoft eyes, a wiggly middle, and a mouth that rests open or gently closed.
  • Try thisKeep the moment predictable. Let your dog approach, sniff, or move away.
  • Don’t assumeA wag only tells you the tail is moving—not whether the feeling is happy.
  • Helpful next stepNotice what changed just before the body changed. Context is the missing clue.
The whole-body check

Read the sentence, not one word.

Dogs speak in clusters of signals. Scan from face to feet, then add the setting: who is nearby, what just happened, and whether your dog can leave.

  • 01
    EarsForward can mean interest; pinned back can mean worry, appeasement, or simply a breeze.
  • 02
    EyesSoft and blinking is different from a fixed stare or showing the whites.
  • 03
    MouthA loose open mouth is not the same as a tight mouth, lip lick, or repeated yawn.
  • 04
    BodyLoose curves invite connection; freezing or leaning away asks for more space.
  • 05
    TailHeight, speed, and stiffness matter together. A wag is not a universal “yes.”
  • 06
    ChoiceThe clearest clue: can your dog move closer, move away, or take a break?
A kind default

When in doubt, make space.

Distance is information. Give your dog a clear exit, lower the intensity, and notice whether their body softens. If they keep escalating or seem unwell, contact a qualified force-free professional or veterinarian.

1
Pause the pressureStop reaching, hugging, cornering, or asking for more.
2
Open an exitTurn your body sideways and give your dog room to choose.
3
Listen to the changeSoftening means the space helped. If not, add more distance.
